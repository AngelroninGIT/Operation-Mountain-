The Mountain

Gym · 60–70 minutes · Once a week

Workout checklist

Before lifting · 5 minutes

Move gently first; save long held stretches for afterwards.

  1. Brisk walk or easy march — 2 minutes.
  2. Arm circles and shoulder rolls — 30 seconds each.
  3. Standing trunk turns — 10 gentle turns, without forcing your back.
  4. Bodyweight squats — 10 slow reps.
  5. Do a light practice set of bench press, row and leg press before working sets.
After lifting · 4 minutes
  1. Doorway chest stretch — 20 seconds each side.
  2. Overhead lat stretch (hands on sturdy support, hinge gently) — 20 seconds.
  3. Cross-body shoulder stretch — 20 seconds each side.
  4. Standing hip-flexor stretch — 20 seconds each side.
  5. Standing quad or calf stretch — 20 seconds each side.
Start with a 5-minute warm-up. Rest 90–120 seconds between most sets. For your first two weeks back, perform just 1–2 sets of each exercise and keep the weights manageable.

Viking Arms

Wednesday · Home · 15–20 minutes

Checklist

Before Viking Arms · 3 minutes

  1. March in place — 60 seconds.
  2. Arm circles and shoulder rolls — 30 seconds each.
  3. Wall push-ups — 8 easy reps.
  4. Gentle wrist circles — 20 seconds.
Afterwards · 3 minutes
  1. Doorway chest stretch — 20 seconds each side.
  2. Cross-body shoulder stretch — 20 seconds each side.
  3. Gentle triceps stretch overhead — 20 seconds each side.
  4. Forearm/wrist stretch — 15 seconds each side.

Warrior Conditioning

Saturday · Home · 15–20 minutes

Checklist

Before Warrior Conditioning · 3 minutes

  1. Easy march — 60 seconds.
  2. Hip circles — 10 in each direction.
  3. Slow bodyweight squats — 8 reps.
  4. Alternating shallow reverse lunges — 5 each side.
Afterwards · 3 minutes
  1. Standing hip-flexor stretch — 20 seconds each side.
  2. Standing quad stretch (hold a support) — 20 seconds each side.
  3. Calf stretch against wall — 20 seconds each side.
  4. Gentle hamstring stretch — 20 seconds each side; keep back neutral.
No equipment needed. For push-ups, use a secure counter or sturdy bench to make them easier if necessary. Keep at least a day between workouts.

Stretch & recovery

Gentle movement for a stiff lower back · about 5–7 minutes

Try these on a mat or firm bed at a comfortable range. Slow breathing, no bouncing or forcing. They may ease stiffness, but do not treat every cause of back pain.

Lower-back comfort routine · muscles beside the spine

For tightness around the two long muscles either side of the lumbar spine (often the erector spinae). Try this once daily or after a workout; it should feel gentle, not painful.

  1. Pelvic tilts — 8 reps. Lie on your back, knees bent and feet supported. Gently flatten your lower back into the mat; release slowly.
  2. Knee rolls — 5 each way. On your back with knees bent, slowly let both knees drift a little side to side; keep shoulders down.
  3. Single knee-to-chest — 5 each side. Gently draw one knee towards your chest, pause 2–5 seconds, then switch. Skip if uncomfortable.
  4. Cat–camel — 6–8 reps. On hands and knees, gently round then relax your back through a comfortable range.
  5. Supported rest position — 30–60 seconds. Lie on your back with knees bent and feet resting on a chair or cushion, breathe slowly and let the muscles beside your spine relax. Skip if it feels worse.
  6. Easy walk — 2–5 minutes. Optional; aim for gentle movement rather than staying in bed.

Start with fewer repetitions if your back is sore. Some mild pulling is okay, but stop any move that increases pain or causes pain shooting down a leg.

When to get help

If back pain persists, keeps recurring, or worsens, see a GP or physiotherapist. Seek urgent medical care for new bladder/bowel control problems, numbness around the groin, or new significant leg weakness. Seek prompt help for fever or severe worsening pain.

The game plan

Big chest, broad shoulders, thick arms, strong back and a solid torso. The goal is steady muscle and strength gain, not a six-pack.

How to lift

  • Use weights you can control with good form.
  • Finish most sets feeling you could do 1–2 more good reps.
  • When all sets reach the top of the rep range, increase the weight a little next time.
  • Begin with reduced sets for the first two weeks.

Food and recovery

A practical daily protein target is about 170–200 g, spread across meals. Aim to recover well and gradually get stronger; there's no need for a big calorie surplus.

Optional upgrade

Adjustable dumbbells would add proper home rows, curls, shoulder presses and farmer's carries. Creatine monohydrate (3–5 g daily) is an optional, well-studied supplement.

Exercise photos

Photos are visual references, not full technique instructions. For unfamiliar movements, ask a qualified gym instructor to check your form.

Safety

Don't rush heavy loads after time away from training. Stop if you feel sharp pain, dizziness or chest discomfort. Seek individual medical advice before vigorous training if you have relevant health concerns.
